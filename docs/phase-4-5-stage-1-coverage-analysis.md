# Phase 4.5 Stage 1: Course-Combination Coverage Analysis

## Current bank coverage

The current question bank contains **19 Biology**, **18 Mathematics**, and **16 English** questions. It contains no Chemistry, Physics, Economics, Literature in English, Government, or Geography questions. This compares the reviewed 46-course onboarding reference only; it is not a national enrolment estimate.

| Subject | Current questions | Mentioned in reviewed combinations | Coverage finding |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| English Language | 16 | Compulsory in every UTME combination | Available, but far below a full mock-volume bank. |
| Mathematics | 18 | 30 courses | Available; valuable for engineering, computing, science, environment, and management. |
| Biology | 19 | 21 courses | Available; supports many medical, agricultural, and science combinations. |
| Chemistry | 0 | 32 courses | **Highest missing-subject priority** in this reference set. |
| Physics | 0 | 31 courses | Co-priority with Chemistry; indispensable for medicine and engineering pathways. |
| Economics | 0 | 14 courses | Next commercial/social-science expansion candidate. |
| Geography | 0 | 11 courses | Important for environment, planning, geology, and some social-science routes. |
| Literature in English | 0 | 9 courses | Essential for arts, law, and several communication routes. |
| Government | 0 | 9 courses | Essential for law, politics, communications, and social sciences. |

## Recommended content order

Chemistry should be the **next subject added**, narrowly ahead of Physics in the representative course map. This is a directional prioritisation rather than a claim about national course demand: the count includes both fixed subjects and permitted alternatives. Chemistry and Physics should be planned as a paired STEM release, because Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy, Biochemistry, and the core Engineering disciplines require both. Economics is the next commercial/social-science candidate; Literature in English and Government should follow to make the course-based flow useful for Law, Arts, and Communication students.

| Release priority | Subject | Reason |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Chemistry | Appears in 32 of 46 reviewed baseline combinations and is currently absent. |
| 2 | Physics | Appears in 31 of 46 reviewed baseline combinations and is paired with Chemistry in core health and engineering pathways. |
| 3 | Economics | Extends management, accounting, business, and economics routes. |
| 4 | Literature in English | Enables core Law, Arts, and Communications pathways. |
| 5 | Government | Completes common Law, Politics, and Communication pathways. |

## Product implication for later stages

Stage 2 should treat the course record as a **recommendation and readiness signal**, not as a guarantee of eligibility. For each derived subject, the dashboard should distinguish **available practice** from **coming soon**, while the current JAMB IBASS course-and-institution record remains the source for final student-facing entry verification.
